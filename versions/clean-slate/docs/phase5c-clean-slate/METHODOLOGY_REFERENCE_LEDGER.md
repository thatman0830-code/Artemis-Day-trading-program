# Phase 5C clean-slate methodology reference ledger

Public methodological references used by the independent first drafts and coordinator synthesis. References support general methodological propositions only; none proves that a specific choice is optimal for this BOT 2.0 object or supplies trading/profitability evidence.

| Ref | Source | Proposition used / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| R01 | Tashman (2000), [Out-of-sample tests of forecasting accuracy](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-2070(00)00065-0) | Forecast-origin, fixed/rolling window, coefficient update, and multiple test-period choices must be specified; rolling origins are a recognized time-series evaluation design. Does not choose this experiment's dates or fit schedule. |
| R02 | Cerqueira, Torgo & Mozetič (2020), [Evaluating time series forecasting models](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-020-05910-7) | Empirical comparison of time-series performance-estimation methods, including temporally ordered out-of-sample methods. Results do not establish a universal split. |
| R03 | Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo (2018), [A note on the validity of cross-validation for autoregressive time series prediction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csda.2017.11.003) | Validity of ordinary K-fold depends on time-series/model assumptions; random folds are not automatically valid here. |
| R04 | Hayashi & Yoshida (2005), [On covariance estimation of non-synchronously observed diffusion processes](https://doi.org/10.3150/bj/1116340299) | Nonsynchronous observations require explicit alignment treatment; it does not mandate one classifier-specific ES/NQ join rule. |
| R05 | Kaufman et al. (2012), [Leakage in Data Mining](https://doi.org/10.1145/2382577.2382579) | Formalizes data leakage and motivates point-in-time feature/transform provenance controls. |
| R06 | Anderson & Goodman (1957), [Statistical Inference about Markov Chains](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177707039) | Foundations for finite-state transition-count estimators; does not imply market labels follow a Markov process. |
| R07 | Scikit-learn, [LogisticRegression documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression.html) | Documents a conventional regularized multinomial linear classifier implementation. Does not justify adding it to the frozen experiment set. |
| R08 | Guo et al. (2017), [On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) | Describes scalar temperature scaling as a post-hoc calibration method; empirical results were on other datasets and do not guarantee fit here. |
| R09 | Gneiting & Raftery (2007), [Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437) | Proper scoring rules for probabilistic forecasts, including categorical forecasts; scores answer a distinct question from hard-label accuracy. |
| R10 | Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery (2007), [Probabilistic forecasts, calibration and sharpness](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x) | Distinguishes calibration and sharpness; supports separate reliability diagnostics. |
| R11 | Brier (1950), [Verification of forecasts expressed in terms of probability](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1950)078%3C0001%3AVOFEIT%3E2.0.CO%3B2) | Original probability forecast verification score. |
| R12 | Geifman & El-Yaniv (2017), [Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html) | Selective classification studies the risk/coverage tradeoff; does not supply this project's threshold. |
| R13 | El-Yaniv & Wiener (2010), [On the Foundations of Noise-free Selective Classification](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/el-yaniv10a.html) | Selective prediction foundations; does not identify an appropriate BOT 2.0 coverage target. |
| R14 | Diebold & Mariano (1995), [Comparing Predictive Accuracy](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599) | Paired predictive-accuracy comparison using a broad set of losses and serially correlated errors under assumptions; not a blanket authorization for a test. |
| R15 | Künsch (1989), [The Jackknife and the Bootstrap for General Stationary Observations](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176347265) | Block resampling methods for dependent stationary sequences under stated regularity conditions. |
| R16 | Newey & West (1987), [A Simple, Positive Semi-Definite, Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent Covariance Matrix](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913610) | HAC covariance estimation under regularity conditions; not a cure for arbitrary dependence. |
| R17 | Romano & Wolf (2005), [Stepwise Multiple Testing as Formalized Data Snooping](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00615.x) | Stepwise familywise-error control with joint dependence considered under method assumptions. |
| R18 | White (2000), [A Reality Check for Data Snooping](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152) | Formalizes inference after model specification search and the risk of selecting a favorable result. |
| R19 | Bailey et al. (2017), [The Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2016.322) | Addresses selection risk across investment backtests; not evidence of this model's quality and not a substitute for chronology. |
| R20 | Cawley & Talbot (2010), [On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) | Model-selection overfitting can bias subsequent performance estimates. |
| R21 | Varma & Simon (2006), [Bias in error estimation when using cross-validation for model selection](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91) | Reusing CV for model selection and performance estimation biases error estimates; nested evaluation is one control in the studied setting. |
| R22 | Sokolova & Lapalme (2009), [A Systematic Analysis of Performance Measures for Classification Tasks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2009.03.002) | Classification metrics have differing properties; endpoint choice is a scientific/design decision. |
| R23 | Demšar (2006), [Statistical Comparisons of Classifiers over Multiple Data Sets](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v7/demsar06a.html) | Multiple-dataset classifier comparisons require appropriate paired/statistical treatment; does not make ES/NQ or WF cells independent replicates. |

No social-media trading advice or claimed trading profitability is used as scientific authority.
