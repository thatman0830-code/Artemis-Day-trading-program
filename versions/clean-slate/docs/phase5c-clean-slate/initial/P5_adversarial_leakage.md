## Purpose and scope

This is a red-team review of how a future comparison could be made to favor any of the declared A0, A1, or A2 implementations without changing their architectures. It proposes controls and audit evidence; it does not establish that any model performs better, settle scientific authority, or authorize evaluation.

The inventory identifies ES and NQ; WF1–WF4 identifiers but no dates or split semantics; 5-, 15-, and 30-minute target horizons; three target heads; A0 persistence, training-majority, and transition-matrix implementations; one A1 MLP; one A2 causal TCN; and six ablation families. It also identifies raw-event fields for event and receive timestamps, a receive-time-availability flag, timestamp source, session and contract identifiers, and sequence/event identifiers. These identities inform the threat model only. Eligibility, prediction-time semantics, split boundaries, target availability, metric definitions, and aggregation weights remain unspecified in the inventory and must be fixed by an authorized protocol before any evaluation.

## Red-team threat model

Assume an analyst can choose among plausible row filters, missing-data rules, prediction/abstention rules, metrics, aggregation schemes, split details, random seeds, and ablations after seeing model behavior. The analyst may do so intentionally or through apparently reasonable convenience choices. The adversary’s objective is to change the apparent ordering of A0, A1, and A2. A0 can be advantaged by easy persistence-heavy or low-entropy populations and by pooled metrics dominated by favorable cells. A1 or A2 can be advantaged by selecting complete, dense, stable sequences or one favorable seed, horizon, instrument, or ablation. Any of them can be advantaged by leakage, unequal denominators, or post-selection.

The controlling principle is to define the forecast information set and eligible population first, freeze a paired comparison cohort and its accounting, and report every prespecified cell and coverage denominator. Out-of-sample forecasting guidance emphasizes explicit origins, periods, update rules, and multiple test periods; model selection on a noisy criterion can itself overfit; and probabilistic forecasts require both calibration diagnostics and proper scores. Those references motivate the controls below, but they do not decide this experiment’s protocol. [Tashman (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-2070(00)00065-0), [Cawley & Talbot (2010)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html), [Gneiting & Raftery (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437).

## Attack register and controls

Each attack below states a mechanism, a deterministic defense to prespecify, audit evidence, and residual risk. “Deterministic” means a rule can be applied identically and replayed from frozen inputs and metadata; it does not mean the proposed rule has already been adopted.

### A. Population, labels, and denominators

| Attack (possible favored system) | Mechanism | Proposed deterministic defense | Required audit evidence | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Favor A0 with a persistence-rich row subset; favor A1/A2 with sequence-friendly rows.** | Select dates, classes, sessions, or rows after observing where a model looks good. A persistence baseline can benefit from long same-state runs. Sequence models can benefit from stable, dense periods. Dropping boundary or transition cases selectively changes difficulty. | Before outcomes are examined, define one eligibility predicate per instrument × horizon × head using only information available at the forecast origin and fixed source-quality criteria. Build and freeze the common eligible row key set. Score all compared systems on the intersection of rows where every required system can issue its defined forecast. Also report the full eligible population and each system’s availability/coverage separately. No metric-specific row deletion. | Versioned eligibility specification; canonical sorted row-key lists and hashes per cell; inclusion/exclusion counts by reason and cell; paired row-level prediction ledger; proof that eligibility inputs exclude targets, model predictions, and post-origin fields. | A common intersection can shrink the target population and favor systems with particular data requirements. Show common-cohort and full-cohort coverage side by side; the former is a paired comparison, not a universal deployment estimate. |
| **2. Use target-dependent eligibility or asymmetric target completion.** | Remove rows whose future labels are difficult to construct, or retain them for some model/cell but not another. A label-complete subset may disproportionately remove gaps, halts, or volatile periods. | Define labelability using a single target-generation contract independent of all model identities. Label failure is a cohort exclusion with a fixed reason code applied before model forecasts are inspected. At each cell, every system uses identical outcome-valid row keys; unavailable labels are never converted to an easy class. | Target-validity table keyed by forecast row, horizon, and head; exact exclusion reasons; counts and rates before/after labelability; reconciliation from eligible forecasts to scored pairs; target builder/configuration identity. | The chosen target-generation contract can still underrepresent severe data outages or disrupted sessions; report exclusions and treat conclusions as conditional on the frozen observable population. |
| **3. Let missingness select the winner.** | Complete-case deletion can remove difficult asynchronous periods and favor a network that requires complete 8×24 inputs; permissive imputation can favor a model that benefits from carried-forward values; model-specific samples defeat pairing. | Freeze missingness semantics per feature and prediction origin. Do not forward-fill from future observations. Where an input sequence is incomplete, use a prespecified model-independent row rule or a prespecified missingness representation available to all systems. Keep “ineligible because input missing” distinct from “model abstained” and “label unavailable.” Report per-feature, per-row, and per-cell missingness and coverage. | Feature-availability masks; per-origin source-age/lag; missingness reason counts; the identical input-row key set; imputation parameters fit on training data only and hashed per fold, if imputation is part of the authorized method; no-future-fill checks. | Missingness may be informative about market state even when handled symmetrically. Missingness-stratified summaries can reveal sensitivity but are not independent confirmatory tests unless declared in advance. |
| **4. Change denominator or weighting to reward favorable cells.** | Pool every row so high-volume instrument/horizon/fold cells dominate; use class-weighted or macro metrics selectively; drop undefined classes or folds; report only nonempty denominators. | Declare a metric dictionary and denominator rules before evaluation. For each head and each WF × instrument × horizon cell, publish integer counts, class supports, confusion matrix, metric numerator/denominator, and missing/undefined status. Use a fixed macro hierarchy (e.g., equal weight per eligible cell within a head) if a summary is authorized; also publish micro/row-pooled values as descriptive sensitivity, never interchangeable. Do not silently omit cells with absent classes or zero denominator. | Row-level prediction/outcome ledger; machine-readable metric definitions; reproducible cell roll-up table; reconciliation that cell denominators sum to paired scored rows; explicit `NA` reasons. | Equal cell weighting estimates a different target than row weighting. Both answer different questions; no aggregation can remove dependence among adjacent forecast origins. |
| **5. Exploit abstention or unequal coverage.** | Score only confident predictions, tune a confidence cutoff on evaluation labels, or allow one system to abstain without counting lost coverage. This can make any model appear accurate on a selected subset; a forced-label A0 may be compared unfairly with a reject-option neural system. | Freeze whether abstention exists and its action/penalty before evaluation. If abstention is allowed, report coverage on the entire frozen eligible denominator and selective risk at prespecified operating points. Primary paired metric must either score an explicit abstention outcome/cost common to all systems or compare at a prespecified matched-coverage rule fitted only on training/validation data. Never discard abstentions from the denominator. | Per-row status (`forecast`, `abstain`, `unavailable`, `invalid`); threshold provenance; coverage numerator/denominator per cell; calibration and threshold fit partition IDs; threshold hashes; score reconciliation including abstentions. | Matched coverage depends on a chosen target coverage and threshold procedure. Report the complete prespecified coverage-risk curve descriptively, with no post-hoc “best point” claim. |

### B. Forecast probabilities, calibration, and decision rules

| Attack (possible favored system) | Mechanism | Proposed deterministic defense | Required audit evidence | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **6. Choose class decisions or probability scores after seeing which favors A0/A1/A2.** | Argmax accuracy can favor sharp but poorly calibrated probabilities; log loss/Brier can rank models differently; class thresholds or tie handling can be changed to flatter a selected model. | Freeze primary estimand and scoring rules per head. If probabilities are available/required, use a strictly proper multiclass score with fixed class order and numerical conventions, alongside calibration diagnostics and a separately declared classification metric. Freeze argmax and exact-tie behavior. Do not switch the primary metric by head, horizon, or observed winner. [Gneiting & Raftery (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437) discusses proper scoring rules; [Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x) discusses calibration and sharpness. | Metric/decision-rule registry with versions; ordered class labels; per-row raw probability vectors and derived decisions; score implementation identity; calibration-bin edges fixed in advance and per-bin counts; deterministic tie tests/specification. | Calibration summaries can be noisy in small cells and bin-dependent. Publish counts and avoid treating a single calibration plot or one binning as definitive. |
| **7. Calibrate or tune on test outcomes.** | Choose temperature, class thresholds, confidence cutoffs, or calibration bins using the same WF evaluation outcomes then present tuned scores as out-of-sample. | If calibration or decision tuning is permitted, fit it solely on an explicitly designated past-only training/validation segment, using a nested/rolling procedure that respects label availability. Apply frozen parameters to the next evaluation segment. If no such segment is specified, omit fitted calibration from confirmatory scoring and report raw probabilities only. | For each fold/cell, calibration fit data keys and cutoff; fitted parameters and hash; temporal cutoff; label-availability proof; immutable mapping from fit partition to evaluation partition. | Nested tuning consumes data and may be unstable when classes are scarce. A calibration procedure chosen after viewing all evaluation folds remains a researcher degree of freedom even if its parameters were fit within folds. |

### C. Splits, time dependence, and purge/embargo

| Attack (possible favored system) | Mechanism | Proposed deterministic defense | Required audit evidence | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **8. Overlap labels or train across a test boundary (often favors flexible A1/A2).** | With 5/15/30-minute forward labels, training outcomes can share future price path with evaluation outcomes if rows near boundaries are retained; random row splits can put near-duplicate windows on both sides. Conversely, a too-wide undocumented purge can remove difficult test rows. | Define each example’s information interval from its actual feature-window start through forecast origin and its label interval from forecast origin through the exact target end/label-finalization time. Use chronological walk-forward evaluation unless a different design is justified. For every fold/horizon/head, purge any training example whose information or target interval overlaps the test interval or uses information unavailable at the training cutoff. Apply a fixed embargo at least as long as the maximum target horizon plus any documented target-finalization latency where intervals otherwise touch; compute it in exchange-time with explicit boundary inclusivity. The inventory names WF1–WF4 but provides no dates or semantics, so do not infer them here. | Fold manifests listing train/validation/test origin keys and exact time bounds; interval intersection report proving zero forbidden overlap; purge/embargo row counts by reason; horizon-specific checks; code/config/hash identity and reviewable fold timeline. | Market dependence can persist beyond the maximum target interval; embargo removes direct overlap, not all serial dependence or regime dependence. The embargo duration and the estimand require prior scientific authorization. |
| **9. Tune split windows, update policy, or fold definitions after seeing results.** | Move WF cutoffs or choose rolling versus expanding windows to obtain more convenient periods for one model; freeze one model while updating another. | Publish exact date/time boundaries, timezone, inclusive/exclusive conventions, window type, refit cadence, feature/target cutoff, and data-update policy for every WF before evaluation. Apply identical origins and target rows to A0, A1, and A2. Keep WF results disaggregated before any fixed roll-up. Tashman’s review identifies these implementation choices as material features of out-of-sample tests. | Signed/hashed fold manifest; per-fold training cutoff and model-fit timestamps; row-to-fold assignment table; per-system fit/update record; audit that all models received the same origins. | Four named folds may not represent future regimes or establish independent replications. Inference must account for temporal clustering and should not treat folds as independent IID datasets. |
| **10. Let session, contract, or roll boundaries leak state.** | Carry sequence features, labels, or transition state across overnight/session gaps or contract roll; use future-known contract/session mapping; place the same continuous sequence or event on both sides of a fold boundary. This may especially favor A0 transition/persistence or sequence models. | Freeze session calendar, session identifier semantics, contract mapping, and roll rules based only on information available at origin. Reset sequence/state at predeclared boundaries where a live forecaster would reset; otherwise define the permitted carry. Forbid future contract identifiers or revised roll labels from features. Treat session/contract boundary intervals explicitly in eligibility and purging. | Per-origin session/contract IDs and provenance; boundary/reset flags; sequence source-key trace; fold-boundary overlap audit; evidence contract mapping was known at forecast origin; exclusion counts around transitions. | Correct handling depends on intended deployment behavior and actual data contract; a reset policy cannot repair inaccurate or retrospectively revised source identifiers. |

### D. Causal availability and timestamp integrity

| Attack (possible favored system) | Mechanism | Proposed deterministic defense | Required audit evidence | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **11. Future label or label-derived feature leakage.** | Target or a component derived from future price movement is joined into features; normalization, feature selection, or state encoding is fit using future/test data; A0 transition counts accidentally include evaluation labels. | Define the forecast information set at origin (t). Every feature and fitted transform must be reproducible from records received and available by (t), with all learned transforms fitted only on that fold’s training data. Freeze targets separately; keep target fields and any future-window aggregates inaccessible to feature construction. A0 transition/majority statistics must be estimated on the permitted training history only. Leakage generally means learning from information not legitimately available for prediction. [Kaufman et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2382577.2382579). | Data-lineage graph; feature-level source timestamps and availability assertions; train-only transform parameters; key-level target-feature separation checks; A0 statistic counts and source key ranges; immutable as-of snapshots. | A pipeline may leak through undocumented upstream transformations or vendor corrections even when field names appear causal. Provenance audits need source-level lineage, not only feature names. |
| **12. Confuse event time with receipt/availability time.** | A record with an earlier exchange event timestamp but a later receive timestamp is used before it could have arrived; `receive_timestamp_available=false` or unknown provenance is treated as proof of timely availability; corrected/backfilled records are used as if known in real time. | Specify the operational decision clock. For each event, require an availability timestamp supported by provenance and no later than the forecast decision time; reject or flag records whose receipt/availability is missing, ambiguous, or after origin. Never substitute event timestamp for receipt availability without documented source semantics. Preserve original and corrected timestamps and apply a deterministic late-arrival policy. | Event/receive/exchange timestamp triads; `receive_timestamp_available`; `timestamp_source`; event IDs and sequence; as-of replay showing exactly which records entered each 8-row feature window; late/corrected record counts and reasons. | Historical feeds may not preserve true receipt time or clock synchronization uncertainty. Results can only claim the information set represented by retained provenance; missing provenance is not cured by imputation. |
| **13. Synchronize ES/NQ using future-arriving cross-market events.** | A cross-market feature samples the nearest ES/NQ event by event time even if the counterpart had not arrived by the origin, or uses a future synchronized bar/close. This can leak into the declared cross-market feature family and favor A1/A2. | For every cross-market feature, use an as-of join on the receiving/availability clock, bounded by a prespecified staleness tolerance. The join must select only counterpart events available at or before origin; no nearest-future matching or centered windows. Fix tie resolution by timestamp, source sequence, then stable event ID. Evaluate the same origins and synchronization rule for ES and NQ. | Join trace with both instruments’ event and availability timestamps; selected counterpart event IDs; staleness/clock-skew fields; counts of unmatched and stale joins; assertions that source availability ≤ decision time. | Asynchronous feeds, clock drift, and venue-specific delay can still create measurement error; sensitivity to a predeclared staleness bound is descriptive unless separately controlled. |
| **14. Bridge gaps, late data, or feed outages with hindsight.** | Forward-fill through a gap, interpolate using the next observed value, treat delayed records as timely, or silently drop gaps. Gap policy may remove precisely the periods where methods differ. | Define maximum age and continuity rules for each feature window before scoring. A gap never authorizes backward-looking interpolation from a future observation. Choose a deterministic policy: mark the sequence unavailable, use a causally computable missingness representation, or reset according to a fixed session rule. Record late-data arrival separately from exchange-time gaps; retain outage rows in the population accounting. | Gap/outage flags and lengths; source freshness per feature; late-arrival ledger; sequence completeness mask; row eligibility and coverage reconciliation around each gap; proof no interpolation read beyond origin. | Data gaps can be nonrandom and market-state dependent. Excluding them changes the target population; report the excluded share and do not extrapolate conclusions to outages. |

## Cross-cutting controls

1. **Freeze a comparison contract before any scoring.** It should bind the experiment identity, source snapshot/hash, eligibility, target and label-finalization semantics, fold boundaries, purge/embargo rule, input availability policy, metric and denominator rules, model identities, and aggregation hierarchy. The inventory provides identity hashes and schema fields but does not supply these protocol choices.
2. **Use one immutable paired ledger.** One row per forecast origin × instrument × horizon × head × system × seed (where applicable), keyed to a stable origin/event identity. Keep raw inputs/provenance, target, prediction probabilities, decision, status, and every exclusion/abstention reason in distinct fields. Derive all summaries from this ledger.
3. **Report the complete declared grid.** Do not report only the best instrument, horizon, WF, seed, or ablation. Keep A0 variants separate; do not choose the best of its three baselines after viewing evaluation results. Report A1/A2 separately and the declared ablation families separately. Any aggregate must be fixed in advance; include cell counts and a no-winner/tie state.
4. **Control repeated selection.** Fix the seed set, training schedule, stopping rule, ablation contrasts, and primary comparison before evaluation. Report every prespecified run, not a selected seed or ablation. If any choices are made using validation results, keep validation inside the training chronology and preserve a final untouched evaluation period. Repeated model/metric selection inflates apparent performance; nested estimation is one established protection for tuning, though it is not a substitute for temporal validity. [Varma & Simon (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91), [Bailey et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.21314/jcf.2016.322), [Cawley & Talbot (2010)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html).
5. **No implicit winner.** Define numerical precision and deterministic ordering of labels/probabilities. Exact score ties remain ties; they must not be broken by choosing the preferred model, the most favorable seed, or a secondary metric introduced after results are known. If a ranked selection is necessary for a separate purpose, its rule and multiplicity treatment must be declared before outcomes.
6. **Respect dependence in uncertainty statements.** Adjacent origins, overlapping horizons, instruments, and folds are not automatically independent. Any uncertainty method must preserve time blocks and paired origins; do not use row-level IID standard errors. If formal pairwise predictive-accuracy tests are authorized, account for serially correlated loss differences and multiple comparisons; the Diebold–Mariano framework is a relevant primary reference, not a blanket endorsement of a particular test here. [Diebold & Mariano (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599). Comparisons across multiple datasets likewise require care about dependence and multiplicity. [Demšar (2006)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v7/demsar06a.html).

## Minimum evidence package before a result can be reviewed

- Frozen protocol and source-data manifest, including the explicit information clock, fold chronology, label intervals, eligibility, and target-finalization semantics.
- Machine-readable row assignment and paired ledger with stable keys, all statuses, model/seed identity, outputs, targets, and source-time provenance.
- Per-cell denominator reconciliation: eligible → input-available → forecast/abstain → label-valid → scored, with integer counts and exclusion reasons.
- Temporal audit: causal feature as-of proof, train-only fitted transforms, fold overlap/purge/embargo proof, and session/contract/cross-market/gap checks.
- Full grid of prespecified WF × instrument × horizon × head × system (and declared ablations/seeds), with raw counts and fixed metrics. No result-based omission or replacement.
- Reproducibility record: exact configuration and code/data identities, deterministic tie and seed behavior, and summary regeneration from the frozen ledger.

## Limits

This draft is not an adopted protocol, not an assessment of any implementation, and not evidence about observed outcomes. The source inventory does not specify actual split times, feature semantics, target intervals, data availability guarantees, metric goals, class prevalence, intended abstention behavior, or the deployment estimand. Those are unresolved protocol decisions. No proposed numerical policy (including purge/embargo length, staleness tolerance, aggregation weights, calibration method, or tie precision) should be treated as selected until an authorized protocol defines it.
