# R4 — ES/NQ market microstructure context

**Status:** independent specialist first pass completed (R4); coordinator synthesis pending. This is market-description research, not a strategy.

ES has a $50 index multiplier and 0.25-point ($12.50) minimum tick; NQ has a $20 multiplier and the same index-point tick ($5). These unit differences matter for any later cost/risk normalization [S58–S60]. Both trade on CME Globex across nearly 23 hours on most weekdays, with maintenance and holiday exceptions; cash RTH, overnight/ETH, and release windows are not interchangeable samples. Use product calendars and timezone/DST-aware boundaries, never a hard-coded midnight boundary [S29,S31,S63,S64].

Empirical work finds intraday volatility structure, but the shape can vary by period and market state. A 2024 study using round-the-clock ES transaction data from 2005–2020 rejects invariance of intraday volatility calendar effects across subsamples; transfer to NQ and the current market remains unestablished [S62]. CME’s own 2025 liquidity note describes ES cash-open/close activity and an April 2025 liquidity shock; useful descriptive context, but exchange-authored and period-specific [S34]. These are contextual findings, not a directional trading signal. The 2016 Wang–Zhang paper indexed in this registry studies trade-size clustering, not intraday volatility; it is not used to support a volatility-cycle claim [S61].

Order-book data has a separate representational cost. CME MDP Market-by-Price (MBP) aggregates quantity/order count by price level; Market-by-Order (MBO) exposes anonymous individual orders and queue-level structure. These are not interchangeable, and reconstruction requires correct sequencing, book state, definitions, recovery and timestamp semantics. Depth/imbalance features can be highly sensitive to feed gaps and latency. Bar-only OHLCV cannot recover queue position, bid/ask events, or intrabar path.

**Evidence classification:** CME specifications/calendars are **TECHNICAL_CONSTRAINT**; empirical intraday-shape work is **FOUNDATIONAL_EVIDENCE** with limited transfer; CME market notes are **EXCHANGE_AUTHORED_CONTEXT**. ES/NQ correlation or cross-lag is not proof of a stable leader. The literature located is insufficient to claim a robust, time-invariant ES-leading-NQ or NQ-leading-ES effect. If later studied, test only with synchronized exchange and receipt timestamps, contract-aware windows, and causal rolling estimation; do not pick a lag from full-sample results.

Academic prior work uses other periods/instruments; transfer to current ES/NQ is limited.
