## 5. MAJOR objections

**X1-MAJ-01 — Admission ambiguity can turn optional families into mandatory search.**

Documented basis: R20 §8 labels B0–B5 a mandatory baseline ladder and includes GRU OR LSTM, whereas §7 gives recurrence secondary priority and the shortlist describes conditional questions. A recurrent network is a competing hypothesis-bearing learner, not a necessary null control.

Why material: requiring recurrence before evidence of a residual question adds state size, traversal, reset and optimization choices without a demonstrated increment in scientific information.

Remediation: later governance should explicitly distinguish mandatory task-matched controls from optional hypothesis tests. Require a written marginal-information justification for recurrence and every subsequent family. This is a correction to admission interpretation, not a demand to remove recurrence permanently.

**X1-MAJ-02 — “Temporal order” is not identified by an architecture comparison alone.**

Documented basis: the TCN dossier asks whether local causal temporal order adds information; the MLP dossier emphasizes static nonlinear interaction. R20 also requires matched information, which is the appropriate safeguard, but does not resolve the exact comparison.

A flattened lagged MLP and lagged linear model already receive ordered coordinates. A TCN gain over these would support convolutional inductive bias, parameter sharing or optimization under that comparison. A gain over current-bar-only controls could instead reflect extra information. Neither alone isolates the value of order.

Remediation: before making a mechanism claim, separate extra history, ordering, capacity and weight-sharing contrasts. Appropriate controls must preserve causal availability and the intended observation population. Arbitrary temporal shuffling can destroy market structure and is not automatically a valid negative control.

**X1-MAJ-03 — The initial representation/target menu remains scientifically over-complete.**

Documented basis: R20 §§5, 10 and 22 admit raw/engineered/hybrid views, additive ES/NQ context, multi-resolution inputs, several target families and a multitask question. Each is conditional, but the package does not establish which minimal unresolved question earns the next increment.

Why material: an architecture winner can actually be a representation, target or loss winner. Even a small number of trials per branch creates many opportunities to revise the narrative. “Not a Cartesian search” is necessary but does not define an admission rule for sequential branching.

Remediation: later governance should state one primary inferential contrast at a time, its common information set, task-matched controls and conditions for expanding scope. Other outputs can remain descriptive without becoming alternative success criteria. This reviewer does not select that estimand or prescribe an R1 experiment.

**X1-MAJ-04 — Multitask and selective-prediction machinery are insufficiently justified as minimal-core capabilities.**

Documented basis: R20 calls multitask a bounded initial question, includes it in Q4, places selective prediction in Q5, and depicts multitask outputs, uncertainty and abstention in its conceptual architecture.

These are legitimate questions, but none is necessary to establish a first incremental forecast result. Multitask weights, output scales, calibration methods and accepted-set rules create additional selection opportunities. A return/risk aggregate can hide a return-task failure; improved accepted-set loss can come from retaining only easier observations.

Counterevidence: R20 explicitly demands single-task comparisons, per-task evidence, nonselective comparison and accepted/rejected accounting. The objection concerns initial necessity, not absence of safeguards.

Remediation: preserve task-appropriate probabilistic scoring and uncertainty reporting, while treating shared training and learned selection as separately admitted extensions. Uncertainty measurement should not be conflated with a requirement for elaborate uncertainty models.

## 6. MODERATE objections

**X1-MOD-01 — Long-context admission can be either redundant or unfairly self-sealing.**

PatchTST and SSM are deferred unless context has value. If “value” means a simple model must first achieve significance with long history, a genuinely different extraction mechanism could be excluded. Conversely, allowing every failure to justify another family produces an endless rescue search.

Require either prior admissible evidence for a specific dependence hypothesis or one tightly bounded exploratory capability test with a predefined interpretation. Failure of a simple long-context learner is neither proof that context is useless nor permission for unrestricted architecture escalation.

**X1-MOD-02 — Complexity is not reducible to parameter count or fit compute.**

R20 recognizes this, but practical admission must also account for fitted preprocessing, state ownership, reset semantics, calibration, monitoring and implementation maintenance. A small recurrent/SSM model can create more verification obligations than a larger stateless baseline.

This is a prospective accounting concern, not a demonstrated runtime defect. No system was inspected or measured.

**X1-MOD-03 — Redundant representations can masquerade as independent evidence.**

Completed coarse bars derived from the same fine history do not introduce new raw observations. They can offer useful compression or regularization. Likewise, common ES/NQ movements do not supply independent replications.

Claims should separate representation efficiency, extra historical coverage and genuinely additional variables. R20 already acknowledges effective-history matching and correlated markets; the remaining issue is keeping these distinctions explicit in later interpretation.

## 9. Evidence against objections

R20 is substantially more conservative than a simple list of its model names suggests. It already:

- Rejects a full factorial search and requires a common program-wide budget.
- Limits recurrence to GRU OR LSTM and SSM exploration to one representative.
- Requires matched information, forward evidence, seed stability and simpler comparators.
- Defers ensembles, elaborate fusion, latent routing, MoE and autonomous adaptation.
- Treats inadequate evidence as uncertainty rather than proof of no effect.

Public evidence also prevents the skeptic from claiming universal superiority of simple models. PatchTST reports useful patch/channel-sharing designs; S4 demonstrates long-sequence capability; Mamba motivates input-selective state. These establish that additional biases can answer meaningful questions in appropriate domains. [PatchTST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730), [S4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396), [Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752).

Thus the defensible position is conditional admission, not architectural prohibition. Regularization and parameter sharing can sometimes reduce estimation variance; additional parameters do not mechanically imply worse generalization. Conversely, a small model with extensive manual feature/search choices is not scientifically simple.

## 10. ES/NQ transferability considerations

No checked primary source establishes the minimum sufficient architecture for causally available intraday ES/NQ observations.

Three transfer distinctions matter:

1. Predicting persistent levels on generic long-horizon benchmarks differs from predicting short-horizon returns, risk or tails. A model’s benchmark ranking need not transfer across estimands.
2. Many bars do not establish many independent market episodes. Session dependence, overlapping outcomes and changing conditions reduce the evidential value of nominal row counts.
3. Two related instruments do not create two independent replications. Joint learning might regularize shared structure or introduce negative transfer and synchronization selection.

The simplest model can still be misspecified. Failure of ridge or a tiny TCN would support only a bounded statement about the approved information, target and tested process.

Cross-market input, pooled training, shared weights and joint output heads are distinct hypotheses. An ES/NQ model should not receive four forms of complexity merely because all are described as “joint representation.”

Causal simpler representation plus stronger controls may be more informative than a more powerful learner, especially when availability or target identity is unresolved. That is an inferential priority, not a claim that bars contain all useful information.

## 11. Minimum defensible prototype portfolio

This is an adversarial recommendation for later consideration, **not prototype selection, R1 design approval or execution authorization**.

For one subsequently authorized estimand, the minimum informative portfolio is:

| Role | Minimal candidate | Scientific purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Task-matched null | Unconditional/zero-change or appropriate mature persistence reference | Establish the relevant no-incremental-information comparison |
| Regularized linear control | Ridge for a mean-type target, logistic regression for a class-probability target, or another justified linear functional | Test linear information on the common eligible history |
| Compact nonlinear control | One small MLP on a declared common information view | Test nonlinearity without assuming convolution is necessary |
| Single temporal challenger | One extremely small causal CNN/TCN representative | Test whether temporal sharing/bias adds beyond the preceding controls |

Logistic and ridge are alternatives tied to different estimands, not mandatory simultaneous targets. A mean-regression model is not automatically a valid quantile comparator.

Why is logistic regression not enough? It may be enough for the selected probability question; nonlinear interaction is a testable additional claim.

Why is ridge not enough? It may be enough for the selected conditional-mean comparison; nonlinear dependence remains unresolved.

Why is a small MLP not enough? It may be enough; convolution needs to earn its place through a specific sharing/order-related comparison.

Why is one tiny TCN not enough? Alone it cannot establish whether gains required temporal convolution or simply eligible information and nonlinear fitting.

GRU/LSTM, PatchTST and SSM are not required in this minimum. Neither are shared return/risk trunks, cross-attention, learned regimes, ensembles or MoE. This minimum cannot establish that all richer alternatives are ineffective.

## 12. Architectures that appear redundant

“Redundant” means insufficiently distinct for the initial scientific question, not mathematically equivalent.

- **GRU and LSTM together:** largely duplicate the initial gated-memory question. R20 correctly chooses one.
- **GRU/LSTM versus TCN:** potentially redundant under the same bounded history unless compressed state, continuation or another identifiable capability is the actual contrast.
- **S4 and Mamba together:** unnecessary initial duplication before useful long context and a selective-state question are established.
- **PatchTST plus SSM:** can duplicate a generic “long context helps” search. Attention interaction and state efficiency require different admission claims.
- **Multiple TCN variants:** kernel, dilation, residual and width variants can become an architecture search despite one family name.
- **Hybrids:** combine components before complementarity is established.
- **Regime specialists/MoE:** add routing and support requirements where continuous observable context may suffice.
- **Ensembles:** different family labels do not establish complementary forward errors.

A completed multi-timeframe view may test efficient summarization without needing a specialized multiscale encoder. Additive cross-market inputs may test incremental information without separate encoders or cross-attention.

## 13. Complexity-induced false-discovery risks

Architecture search can manufacture apparent alpha by optimizing noisy development results even when every candidate is individually modest. This is a methodological possibility, not a finding that BOT 2.1 has done so. [Cawley and Talbot](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html).

Relevant degrees of freedom include family, context, representation, normalization, target, horizon, loss, regularization, optimizer, checkpoint, seed handling, pooling, calibration, abstention and stopping. The number of independent tests cannot be recovered by multiplying option counts; choices are dependent and adaptive. Nevertheless every outcome-informed branch belongs in the research history.

Sequential escalation is especially dangerous: an unsuccessful simple model motivates longer context; a weak long-context model motivates attention; an unstable attention model motivates an ensemble. Each step can sound reasonable while continually expanding the opportunity to select noise.

Calibration stability and maintenance burden can worsen too. Additional fitted layers require their own development information; accepted-set results can change when either forecasting or selection shifts. Seed shopping can conceal instability, while counting seeds as independent markets exaggerates evidence.

The response is bounded admission, complete logging and appropriately separated evaluation—not simply a small parameter limit.

## 17. Non-blocking concerns

A small TCN may underfit. A simple model may miss interactions. Finite data may leave the comparison inconclusive. These are ordinary scientific uncertainties, not automatic blockers.

The precise eventual framework, precision and operator choices remain outside this review’s empirical scope. SSM systems burden is a conditional concern, not a measured incompatibility.

Descriptive calibration diagnostics remain valuable even when elaborate uncertainty models are deferred. Likewise, carefully labeled descriptive risk forecasts need not be removed merely because a shared multitask trunk is unjustified.

No result should be forced into “complexity won” or “no signal exists” when the evidence supports only a narrower conclusion.

## 18. What R20 got right

R20 resisted several common mistakes: inherited BOT 2.0 architecture authority, modernity as justification, protected-history reuse, calibration as a certificate, ensemble labels as diversification, and feature drift as automatic retraining permission.

It also correctly preserves historical A1/A2 as descriptions rather than runnable baselines; refuses arbitrary numerical thresholds; separates forecast performance from economic usefulness; and maintains research outside trading authority.

Its strongest internal defense against this review is its own requirement for one bounded comparison with matched information and honest elimination. Applying that principle strictly would substantially reduce the objections.

## 19. What R20 may be overclaiming

The risk is overclaiming **necessity**, not explicitly overclaiming profitability:

- A mandatory ladder extending through recurrence implies more required comparison than the evidence supports.
- A “temporal order” label can overstate what an architecture contrast identifies.
- Five broad core questions can understate the cumulative research burden.
- Multitask and selective outputs in a conceptual diagram can become assumed components.
- A primary TCN designation can imply sample-efficiency evidence that has not been established.

R20 itself identifies related limitations. These objections therefore ask later governance to enforce the caveats rather than treating their presence as sufficient protection.

This reviewer makes the corresponding concession: removing complexity is not proof of adequate capacity. A minimum portfolio is a bounded way to learn, not a complete test of market predictability.
