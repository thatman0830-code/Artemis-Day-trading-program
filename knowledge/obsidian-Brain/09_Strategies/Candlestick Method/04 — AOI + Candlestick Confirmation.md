## 1. Core integration principle

- **Direction** determines the side.
- **Structure** establishes the market context.
- **AOI** determines **WHERE** to look.
- **Candlestick confirmation** determines **WHEN** an entry may become valid.

Source's core distinction (preserved verbatim, not strengthened): **"Don't trade candlestick patterns. Trade price action at important areas, using candlestick patterns as confirmation."** Per the source, the candlestick pattern is *"Step 3 — the entry trigger, not the entire trading strategy."*

*(This note does not claim candlestick confirmation improves profitability — see §11 Validation Boundary.)*

## 2. Direction → Structure → AOI → Candlestick Confirmation

The **existing Trading Course Method is the governing operational framework** for Direction, Structure, AOI, invalidation, and target/R:R. The candlestick layer is **subordinate** to: current market evidence, the Master Script, regime analysis, top-down structure, the predefined AOI, decision authority, structural invalidation, and target/R:R requirements. Candlestick confirmation only occupies the entry-signal slot **after** Direction → Structure → AOI are satisfied.

## 3. AOI / key-level requirement

The source (§12) requires: **don't chase — wait for price to return to an important level**, then look for candlestick confirmation there. Source examples of key levels:
- support
- resistance
- trendline
- dynamic support/resistance
- Fibonacci level
- other important price-action areas

**Authority distinction:** the **Trading Course Method's existing AOI rule remains authoritative** (areas where buyers/sellers previously reacted; **minimum 3 touches = valid AOI**, restated in §15). The source's broader key-level *types* (trendline, Fibonacci, dynamic S/R) are reference examples.
- **Whether the 3-touch requirement extends to trendlines, Fibonacci levels, or dynamic support/resistance: Not established by the source.** Do not automatically apply 3-touch to those; the existing horizontal-AOI rule governs unless separately established.

## 4. No AOI → no candlestick setup

- A candlestick pattern appearing in the middle of nowhere is **not** a setup.
- A pattern outside a predefined AOI does **not** create an entry.
- A pattern **cannot manufacture** an AOI.
- Direction alone is **insufficient**.
- Candlestick confirmation only becomes relevant **after price reaches the area of interest.**

Source (§15): **"No AOI → No entry signal. No confirmation → No trade."**

## 5. Confirmation > anticipation (§14)

- **Wait for the candle to close.**
- Do **not** treat an unfinished candle as confirmation.
- A candle can change dramatically before its timeframe closes ("a candle that looks bullish halfway through its timeframe could finish bearish").
- Do **not** anticipate the signal. Source: **"The candle close is the confirmation."**

This preserves the source's framing; it is **not** a newly invented Brain rule (the candle-close rule already exists in [[Trading Course Method/03 — Entry & Confirmation]]).

## 6. The six patterns as confirmation tools

The six patterns (Engulfing, Pin Bar, Three-Bar Continuation, Three-Bar Reversal, Breakout, Shrinking) occupy the **confirmation / entry layer**, used **only** after Direction → Structure → AOI. Their catalogue is not reproduced here — see [[Candlestick Method/03 — Six Candlestick Patterns]]. Structures are A; their meaning/reliability is C (unvalidated).

## 7. Higher-timeframe guidance (B — contextual)

Source (§12): the instructor **recommends** focusing on **1-hour charts and above**, because candlestick patterns are **"generally considered less reliable on very low time frames."**
- Classified **B** (preference/contextual guidance).
- Higher-timeframe patterns are **not** presented as proven superior. **Comparative reliability across timeframes is unvalidated** (C).

## 10. A / B / C classification

- **A (explicit):** the Direction → AOI → candlestick-confirmation hierarchy as the source presents it; the requirement to **wait for the candle close**; the **pattern-alone-is-not-enough** principle.
- **B (preference / contextual):** the higher-timeframe preference; the source's specific key-level examples beyond the existing authoritative AOI framework (trendline/Fib/dynamic S/R); any instructor preference around confirmation.
- **C (requires validation — do not convert to rules):** whether candlestick confirmation improves outcomes; whether patterns at AOIs outperform patterns elsewhere; whether candle-close confirmation improves expectancy; whether higher-timeframe patterns are more reliable; whether any particular pattern has an edge.

## 11. Validation boundary

**This note does not establish that candlestick confirmation has an edge.** Future validation *may* eventually examine: pattern + AOI vs. AOI without pattern; individual pattern performance; timeframe; regime; confirmation-after-close vs. anticipation; confluence; resulting R-multiple. **No validation log or schema field is created in this step.**

## 12. Explicit non-overrides

Candlestick confirmation does **NOT** override: the Master Script; current market evidence; regime rules; cross-asset evidence; no-trade conditions; AOI requirements; structural invalidation; R:R requirements; the Decision Capture Protocol; or user decision authority.
