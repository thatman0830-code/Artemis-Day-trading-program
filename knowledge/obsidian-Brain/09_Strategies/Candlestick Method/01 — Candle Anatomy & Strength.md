## Summary

What a candlestick shows (OHLC), its parts (body, wick/shadow), the bullish/bearish definition, and the source's reading of candle "strength" from body size. Detailed wick interpretation is covered separately in `02 — Wick Interpretation`.

## A — Course/Trader Rules (explicit definitions)

- **What a candle is:** a way of displaying price history for a specific period (e.g., 1-minute candle = 1 minute of price action; 1-hour = 1 hour; daily = 1 day).
- **OHLC** — unlike a line chart (which primarily shows closing prices), a candlestick gives four pieces of information:
  - **Open** — where the period started.
  - **High** — highest price reached.
  - **Low** — lowest price reached.
  - **Close** — where the period ended.
  - The source states this "reveals how price moved during the candle, which is extremely important for price-action trading."
- **Body:** shows the distance between the open and close.
- **Wicks / Shadows:** show how far price traveled beyond the opening/closing range. *(Interpretation of wicks → `02 — Wick Interpretation`.)*
- **Bullish candle:** **Close > Open** — the source states this "indicates buyers had more control during the period."
- **Bearish candle:** **Close < Open** — the source states this "indicates sellers had more control during the period."

## B — Instructor Preference / contextual guidance (candle-strength reading)

The source reads "strength" from body size, using hedged/tendency language:

- **Large body:** a large bullish body "**generally** indicates strong buying pressure"; a large bearish body "**generally** indicates strong selling pressure." "The larger the body, the more decisive the movement **generally** was."
- **Small body:** "indicates less directional dominance"; "Buyers and sellers were competing, and neither side was able to move price significantly." *(Source interpretation: indecision / competition.)*

These are the source's contextual readings, not universal rules.

## C — Requires Validation

- Whether candle-strength read from body size has **predictive / effectiveness value** in my own trading is **not established by the source** and is **unvalidated**.
- The interpretive descriptions ("buyers/sellers had more control," "more decisive," "less directional dominance") are the source's stated interpretations, **not** proven facts about future price behavior. Their reliability requires independent validation.
