## RQ1 — Does crypto reliably fail as a haven on geopolitical risk-off days?

- **Research question:** On geopolitical risk-off days, does crypto (BTC/ETH) reliably decline with risk assets rather than act as a safe haven?
- **Originating historical observation:** Observation 2 — "Crypto behaving as macro-beta rather than a haven" ([[Historical Market-Brief Observations (Aug 2026)]]).
- **Source briefs:** [[2026-08-05-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-07-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-08-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-09-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-10-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-11-daily-market-brief]]
- **Historical evidence currently available:** BTC/ETH/SOL flat-to-down while equities set records (8/5); BTC's move above $65K coincided with the jobs print (8/8); BTC −1.52% on the 8/10 geopolitical risk-off day; BTC/ETH opened lower into pre-CPI caution then recovered (8/11). This is essentially **one clean geopolitical risk-off test (Aug 10)** plus supporting macro-beta context.
- **Evidence strength (approved matrix):** RQ derived from a **Strong (within-window)** observation, but as a *predictive* question the direct test is **Weak** — a single geopolitical instance.
- **What the historical briefs claimed:** that crypto "fails as a haven, trades as risk" / is "macro beta, not a haven."
- **Factual / as-reported:** the crypto price moves and their coincidence with the cited macro/geopolitical events, as reported (with the archive's data caveats).
- **Historical AI interpretation:** the causal/behavioral framing that crypto *reliably* behaves as macro-beta rather than a haven.
- **Remains unresolved:** whether this holds across different shock types and regimes; a single geopolitical instance cannot establish reliability.
- **Next Evidence Required:** multiple future geopolitical/risk-off events, in varied regimes, each recording crypto's direction vs. equities and vs. recognized havens (gold, Treasuries) — independent instances, not restatements of one event (§08).
- **Independent confirming instance:** a future geopolitical risk-off day where crypto declines with/ahead of equities while classic havens catch a bid.
- **Contradictory evidence:** a geopolitical risk-off day where crypto rallies as a defensive/haven asset while equities fall.
- **Validation status:** Open — historical origin only; no independent live evidence yet. Requires independent evidence across future market environments before any promotion.

## RQ2 — Does a silver 50-day reclaim mark durable bottoms?

- **Research question:** Does silver reclaiming its 50-day moving average reliably mark a durable bottom?
- **Originating historical observation:** Observation 4 — "Precious-metals recovery from post-bubble lows" ([[Historical Market-Brief Observations (Aug 2026)]]).
- **Source briefs:** [[2026-08-07-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-08-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-09-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-10-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-11-daily-market-brief]]
- **Historical evidence currently available:** the 50-day reclaim metric was **set by the previous workflow** on 8/7, described as pierced intraday 8/8, above on a closing basis 8/9, and extended 8/10 (silver ~$66); ~$65 range on 8/11. This is a **single episode graded against a self-defined metric**.
- **Evidence strength (approved matrix):** derived from a **Moderate** observation whose "bottom confirmed" call was **self-set and self-graded**; as a predictive question the test is **Weak** (one episode).
- **What the historical briefs claimed:** that silver reclaiming its 50-day "signals a bottom," later described as "confirmed."
- **Factual / as-reported:** the silver price path and the moving-average readings, as reported (moving-average values themselves drifted between briefs).
- **Historical AI interpretation:** that the 50-day reclaim *marks a durable bottom* — an interpretation graded against the same workflow's own metric, not independent evidence.
- **Remains unresolved:** durability beyond the window; whether the bottom holds is not established by the archive, and a single self-graded episode cannot establish reliability.
- **Next Evidence Required:** multiple future instances (silver and, separately, other assets) where a 50-day reclaim is recorded and then followed forward to see whether a durable bottom actually formed, judged on an **independent** (non-self-graded) basis across regimes.
- **Independent confirming instance:** a future 50-day reclaim followed by a sustained, multi-month bottom/recovery that was not defined by the same analysis making the call.
- **Contradictory evidence:** 50-day reclaims followed by renewed declines to/through prior lows (failed bottoms).
- **Validation status:** Open — historical origin only; no independent live evidence yet. Requires independent evidence across future market environments before any promotion.

## RQ3 — Does "records-then-fade" recur at resistance?

- **Research question:** Do fresh index highs set near resistance reliably fade intraday (consolidation-at-highs) rather than break out cleanly?
- **Originating historical observation:** Observation 7 — "'Records-then-fade' / consolidation near resistance" ([[Historical Market-Brief Observations (Aug 2026)]]).
- **Source briefs:** [[2026-08-05-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-07-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-08-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-10-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-11-daily-market-brief]]
- **Historical evidence currently available:** fresh intraday records on the S&P/Dow (8/5, and Aug-6 data in the 8/7 brief) followed by fades — the Dow gave back 400+ points from its Aug-6 peak (8/7); S&P record close 7,763.08 (8/8); stalling just under records into CPI (8/10, 8/11). All within **one uptrend/consolidation**.
- **Evidence strength (approved matrix):** derived from a **Moderate** observation; as a predictive question, evidence is confined to a single consolidation episode.
- **What the historical briefs claimed:** "records, then a fade" / "consolidation-at-highs, not a breakdown" / "sellers active right at resistance."
- **Factual / as-reported:** the record prints and subsequent fades, as reported (FMP "year-high" fields were self-flagged unreliable; observed intraday highs are the reference).
- **Historical AI interpretation:** that this is a recurring *behavioral tendency at resistance* rather than an artifact of one consolidation.
- **Remains unresolved:** whether it recurs across different uptrends/resistance tests; how this specific episode resolved past CPI is not in the archive.
- **Next Evidence Required:** multiple future instances, across different uptrends and instruments, recording whether new highs near resistance fade intraday vs. break and hold.
- **Independent confirming instance:** a future new-high-at-resistance day that fades intraday and is followed by consolidation rather than clean continuation — repeated across independent episodes.
- **Contradictory evidence:** new highs at resistance that break out and hold/extend without the fade.
- **Validation status:** Open — historical origin only; no independent live evidence yet. Requires independent evidence across future market environments before any promotion.

## RQ4 — Does "VIX-up-while-index-flat" lead subsequent risk-off behavior?

- **Research question:** When VIX rises on a flat/near-flat index day, does subsequent risk-off behavior reliably follow?
- **Originating historical observation:** Observation 5 — "VIX complacency followed by an early wake-up (VIX-up-while-index-flat)" ([[Historical Market-Brief Observations (Aug 2026)]]).
- **Source briefs:** [[2026-08-03-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-05-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-07-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-08-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-09-daily-market-brief]], [[2026-08-10-daily-market-brief]]
- **Historical evidence currently available:** VIX levels across the window (15.99 → 14.90) plus a **single** up-while-flat instance on 8/10 (VIX +3.56% to 15.43 while the S&P was ~−0.04%). (VIX unavailable in the 8/11 brief.)
- **Evidence strength (approved matrix):** derived from a **Moderate** observation, but the up-while-flat divergence itself is a **single instance** — as a predictive question, **Weak**.
- **What the historical briefs claimed:** "hedging demand rising faster than the tape is falling," a transition "to an early-warning state."
- **Factual / as-reported:** the VIX readings and the flat index on 8/10, as reported.
- **Historical AI interpretation:** that this divergence *leads* subsequent risk-off — a predictive framing built on one data point.
- **Remains unresolved:** whether the signal reliably precedes risk-off; a single instance cannot establish lead/lag reliability, and how it resolved into the pending CPI is not in the archive.
- **Next Evidence Required:** multiple future instances where VIX rises on a flat/near-flat index day, each followed forward a defined number of sessions to record whether risk-off actually materialized — tracking false positives as well as hits.
- **Independent confirming instance:** a future VIX-up-while-index-flat day reliably followed by a risk-off move within a defined window, repeated across independent episodes.
- **Contradictory evidence:** VIX-up-while-flat days **not** followed by risk-off (false signals), or risk-off arriving with no prior up-while-flat tell.
- **Validation status:** Open — historical origin only; no independent live evidence yet. Requires independent evidence across future market environments before any promotion.
